# Appliance bill of materials, 2026-11-14

**Date checked:** 2026-11-14. **Currency:** USD, before tax, shipping, and tariffs. **Scope:** one
headroom-biased Bittern appliance for 16 owned PoE cameras, now mixed 4, 5 and 8 MP, with
14-day base retention and a separately priced multi-drive expansion. Every line must ship
from the United States. The build is a small NAS cube, not a 10-inch rack; the PoE switch and
patch panel move to the wall or a shelf. This is a costed research note for
[OQ-09](../open-questions.md), not a purchase order and not a resolution of OQ-09.

**Verification at the check date:** the Headroom BOM has 16 lines: 14 directly verified
(High), 2 Medium (board and OS NVMe), and 0 Low. The board is the hardest line: it is a new
US-stocked 8-SATA NAS board from a small brand, the same OEM design sold by CWWK, and quality
is the risk, not CPU support. The previous
Hong Kong marketplace MSI board is not used. The 4-SATA board limit in the brief is kept as a
fallback case, not as the primary build. Memory and hard-drive prices are at multi-year highs.

The primary build is the Middle scenario: StoneStorm Q670 8-bay NAS board, 32GB DDR5, one
24TB drive, Hailo-8, 24-port 384W PoE switch, Jonsbo N4 case, and Corsair SF750. The Headroom
scenario adds 64GB memory; the Value scenario takes the 20TB drive and the 16-port 250W
switch. The totals are at the end. The RAM recommendation is 32GB now; 64GB later is a
replace-both upgrade, not a second pair to add.

Requirements come from [ADR 0004](../adr/0004-docker-on-bare-metal-debian.md) for direct
`/dev/dri` access, [ADR 0005](../adr/0005-dual-nic-isolated-camera-network.md) for the dual
NIC roles, [ADR 0007](../adr/0007-mkosi-os-image-and-releases.md) for the Debian image and
hardware assumptions, [ADR 0014](../adr/0014-dedicated-recording-drive.md) for the dedicated
recording drive, [ADR 0018](../adr/0018-recording-capacity-pool.md) for the mergerfs capacity
pool, the [Frigate assessment](frigate-assessment.md) for acceleration and storage
guidance, and the [hardware reconnaissance brief](../plan/session-briefs/p0-hardware-recon.md).
The brief's N100/N305 candidate is below the purchase target in this note; the note keeps the
brief's rule that nothing is claimed as measured until it is measured.

## What changed in this revision

| Change | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 8 MP headroom | Storage and PoE are sized for 16 x 8 MP, not 16 x 4 MP. CPU and accelerator stay because recording is a stream copy and detection uses a substream. |
| 12 Mbps worst case | Storage tables show 8 Mbps average and 12 Mbps worst case. The base drive is chosen for 14 days at 8 Mbps, not 30 days. |
| US sourcing | Every line is US-shipped. The Hong Kong marketplace MSI board is removed. Chinese brands are kept only when a US retailer stocks and fulfills them. |
| Board | StoneStorm Q670 8-bay NAS board replaces the MSI MPG B760I Edge WiFi. It has 8 SATA, 3 M.2, dual i226 2.5G, and two DDR5 DIMMs. |
| Separate NIC | Removed. One onboard i226 2.5G port is `cam0`; the other is `lan0`. |
| PoE switch | A 24-port 384W TP-Link is the headroom choice; the 16-port 250W TP-Link is the value choice. |
| Multi-drive | One 24TB drive is in the base total. Two-drive and three-drive blocks are the normal expansion plan. Capacity scaling is a mergerfs pool decided in ADR 0018; redundancy is still open in OQ-19. The extra drives are priced separately and are not in the base total. |
| HBA growth | The primary 8-SATA board does not need an HBA. A 4-SATA fallback would, so the HBA is priced as an optional item only. |

## US sourcing rule

Every line in the BOM satisfies one of these:

- Amazon, sold and shipped by Amazon, or sold by the brand with Prime/FBA fulfillment.
- Newegg, sold by Newegg, or a marketplace seller that states US shipping.
- B&H, CDW, Best Buy, or a manufacturer's US store.
- A US-based distributor such as Mouser when the part ships from its US warehouse.

No line ships from mainland China or Hong Kong. No AliExpress-style listing is used. Chinese
brands are acceptable only when the stock is already in the US and the retailer fulfills it.
Backorders longer than about two weeks are excluded. The BOM has a "Ship from / seller type"
column so the rule is visible on every line.

## Storage

### Per-day and retention at 8 MP

| Quantity | 8 Mbps per camera | 12 Mbps per camera |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aggregate bitrate, 16 cameras | 128 Mbps | 192 Mbps |
| Write rate | 16 MB/s | 24 MB/s |
| Per day | 1.38 TB | 2.07 TB |
| 14 days | 19.35 TB | 29.03 TB |
| 14 days plus 10 percent | 21.29 TB | 31.93 TB |
| 30 days | 41.47 TB | 62.21 TB |
| 30 days plus 10 percent | 45.62 TB | 68.43 TB |

A single 24TB drive covers roughly 14 days at 8 Mbps and about 10 days at 12 Mbps. It cannot
cover 30 days at 8 MP.

The arithmetic is slightly more generous than the headline: 24TB nominal, less 1 percent
filesystem and 10 percent reserve, is 21.38TB, which is 15.5 days at 8 Mbps and 10.3 days at
12 Mbps. The 14-day figure is the conservative planning number, not a claim that the drive
stops at exactly 14 days.

### Expansion block

This is the mergerfs recording pool decided in ADR 0018. Adding a drive formats only the new
drive; existing members are not reformatted. Redundancy is still open in OQ-19. The extra
drives are a normal expansion plan, not part of the one-drive base total.

| Archive | Drives | Added cost over base | Block price | Retention at 8 Mbps | Retention at 12 Mbps | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base | 1 | 0 | 1,412.99 | 14 to 15 days | about 10 days | One 24TB drive. |

### 20-camera growth case

At 20 cameras and 8 Mbps, the aggregate is 160 Mbps, or 20 MB/s, or 1.73 TB per day. Fourteen
days is 24.19TB before overhead, or 26.61TB with the 10 percent reserve. One 24TB drive covers
about 12.4 days; two drives cover about 24.7 days. A 20-camera 14-day archive therefore needs
the two-drive block, not one drive.

| Cameras | Bitrate | Per day | 14 days | 14 days plus 10 percent | One drive | Two drives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 16 | 8 Mbps | 1.38 TB | 19.35 TB | 21.29 TB | 15.5 days | 30.9 days |
| 20 | 8 Mbps | 1.73 TB | 24.19 TB | 26.61 TB | 12.4 days | 24.7 days |

### SATA and HBA growth limit

The brief assumed a 4-SATA board: four drives work directly, and the N4's fifth and sixth bays
need a PCIe HBA. That HBA would take the only PCIe slot, so the separate NIC would have to
move to the M.2 A+E slot. The sourced StoneStorm board has eight SATA ports through two
included SFF-8643 breakout cables, so the primary build removes that limit. All six N4 bays
work without an HBA, and the PCIe slot stays free.

The 4-SATA path is retained for the fallback board. If the ASUS B760-I fallback is used, four
drives work directly and the fifth and sixth need the optional HBA. The HBA is priced in the
optional table. The M.2 A+E 2.5G NIC adapter needed to free the PCIe slot was not confirmed
US-shipped at the check date, so 5th and 6th drive growth is a future item, not a base feature.

## PoE and switch

| Case | Camera PoE load | Switch self-consumption | AC load at the switch | 250W PoE margin | 384W PoE margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 W per camera | 128 W | about 25 W | about 153 W | 122 W | 256 W |
| 12 W per camera | 192 W | about 25 W | about 217 W | 58 W | 192 W |

The 16-port 250W switch covers 16 cameras at 12W with 58W left, but it has no spare ports and
the margin disappears if a camera boots hard or a 16-port unit is replaced with a PTZ. The
24-port 384W switch leaves 192W and eight spare ports. That is the headroom pick.

The 24-port switch is a 1U, 440mm-wide unit. Width no longer matters because it mounts on the
wall or a shelf, not in a 10-inch rack. Its four SFP uplinks are 1G, which is ample: 192 Mbps
is 19.2 percent of a 1G link.

The UPS is sized on the whole appliance, not just the cameras. At 16 x 12W the switch draws
about 226W and the appliance about 95 to 115W, so the total is about 320 to 340W. The
CP1500PFCLCD is a 1000W unit, but its runtime at that load is roughly 12 to 18 minutes, not
the 30 to 40 minutes it gives at 150W. Measure the runtime before setting the shutdown
threshold.

## CPU and accelerator

Recording is a stream copy, so 8 MP does not add a transcode load. Detection stays at 5 fps on
a low-resolution substream, so it does not scale with the main-stream resolution. The i5-13500
with UHD 770 still has headroom for 16 H.265 streams, and the Hailo-8 still has headroom for
80 inferences per second. The 8 MP change is a storage and network change far more than a CPU
change.

The one place 8 MP can bite is live view. Frigate's UI displays a handful of streams at once,
not 16 4K tiles. If the buyer wants a 16-up 4K live wall, the UHD 770 may not keep up and a
low-profile GPU with hardware decode would be needed. Budget about 150 to 250 USD for a used
or entry-level low-profile card; that estimate is not verified and is not in the BOM.

## Network

| Flow | 16 x 8 Mbps | 16 x 12 Mbps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Camera ingest on `cam0` | 128 Mbps | 192 Mbps |
| 2.5GbE link utilization | 5.1 percent | 7.7 percent |
| 1GbE link utilization | 12.8 percent | 19.2 percent |
| Detection inferences | 80 per second | 80 per second |

The camera side runs at 1G. The 24-port switch's 1G uplink is 19.2 percent utilized at the
12 Mbps worst case, so the switch uplink is not a bottleneck. The 2.5GbE NIC is kept for
`lan0` and future headroom, not because the camera aggregate needs it. One onboard i226 2.5G
port is `cam0`; the other is `lan0`.

## Memory

Frigate's guidance for 16 cameras is well under 64GB. 32GB is the recommendation. 8 MP changes
storage and network far more than memory. 64GB is headroom for a future camera count, a higher
detection resolution, or a heavier Frigate build, not a requirement for 8 MP.

The StoneStorm board has two DDR5 DIMM slots. The Middle and Value scenarios use 2 x 16GB,
which fills both slots. 64GB later means replacing both modules with 2 x 32GB at the 64GB
price and shelving the 16GB pair. There is no second pair to add. The alternative is 1 x 32GB
now plus a second 32GB later, which runs single-channel until the second module arrives. I
would not accept that for this workload: the i5-13500 decode and detect pipeline and the
Frigate database benefit from dual-channel memory, and matching a second 32GB module later is
not guaranteed.

shelf, so its fans are not inside the case.

The CS01-HS fails because it has no 3.5-inch bays.

### Board fit and M.2 layout

The StoneStorm Q670 is a 170 x 170mm Mini-ITX board with a 65W-class LGA1700 socket. Its
cooler clearance with the 37mm NH-L9i in the N4 is not verified; the board has VRM and M.2
heatsinks that could interfere with the 92mm fan overhang. Dry-fit the board and cooler before
buying the case.

| Slot | Location | Interface | Length | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M.2_1 | Front | PCIe 4.0 x4 | 2280 or 22110 | OS NVMe (WD SN770) |
| M.2_2 | Back | PCIe 4.0 x4 | 2280 | Hailo-8 M.2 2280 M-key |
| M.2_3 | Back | PCIe 4.0 x4 | 2280 | Spare |

The Hailo-8 is an M.2 2280 M-key module, which fits the board's M-key slot. Put the OS NVMe on the front slot
and the Hailo-8 on a back slot. The N4's removable top cover gives access to the front M.2,
but I could not confirm from the fetched N4 manual or photos that the back M.2 slots are
reachable without removing the motherboard from the tray. Mark that access unverified. Install
the OS NVMe and Hailo-8 before mounting the board, and check the Hailo temperature because the
back slot has no direct airflow.

### SATA and bay map

The board has eight SATA ports through two SFF-8643 connectors and includes two breakout
cables. The N4's four hot-swap bays need four SATA data cables; the two direct bays need two
more. The remaining two ports are spare. The BOM keeps one 3-pack of Cable Matters SATA cables
for the direct bays or as right-angle spares. If the included breakout cables are too short or
have straight connectors that foul the backplane, use the Cable Matters cables.

## What I could not verify

1. **The board is a small-brand NAS board.** CPU support is confirmed: the Amazon and Newegg
   listings state LGA1700 support for 12th, 13th and 14th gen and name the i5-13500. The real
   risk is quality: this is the same OEM design sold by CWWK, with public reports of PCIe and
   BIOS quirks. Buy from Amazon with its return window, burn-in test before the case goes
   together, and keep the ASUS B760-I fallback. Its compatibility with the 64GB DDR5 kit, fit
   with the NH-L9i in the N4, long-term support, and Debian support for both i226 ports,
   including the i226-LM, are still unverified. The 4-SATA fallback ASUS B760-I is refurbished;
   the listing says US shipping but the seller name and warranty terms were not fully captured.
   The board is the hardest line in the BOM.
2. **The OS NVMe price.** Newegg showed 126.73, sold by Newegg; an earlier fetch showed 164.10.
   The price moved between fetches. Confirm before ordering.
3. **The Hailo-8 starter kit, tariff, and thermals.** Mouser listed the HM218B1C2XAE starter
   kit at 191.25 with 33 in stock at the check date; the kit includes the thermal management
   parts. Confirm the 8 percent US tariff at checkout, and check the module temperature in the
   N4 back slot. The 2242 Waveshare listing is not used because the board's back slots are
   2280.
4. **The 24-port switch stock.** B&H showed 419.99 in stock at the check date. Stock can change
   quickly. The CDW listing is 499.99 and was not used.
5. **Memory and drive prices.** These are multi-year highs and move daily. The 64GB kit is
   1,029.99 and the 32GB kit is 469.99 at Newegg direct; the 24TB drive is 1,412.99 at CDW.
6. **The 20TB SkyHawk AI.** Best Buy showed 719.99; stock was not deeply checked. It is the
   Value primary, not the base.
7. **The 4-SATA growth path.** The HBA is 48.12 at Mouser, but the M.2 A+E 2.5G NIC adapter
   needed to free the PCIe slot was not confirmed US-shipped at the check date. Treat 5th and
   6th drive growth as a future item, not a purchase now.
8. **The 8 SATA breakout cables and controller.** The board includes two SFF-8643 to 4 x SATA
   cables. I assume they are long enough for the N4 backplane and direct bays, and that the
   8-port controller is AHCI, all eight ports enumerate under Debian, and the M.2 slots and
   SATA ports do not share lanes when fully populated. If the cables are too short, add SATA
   extensions or use the Cable Matters pack.
9. **4K live view.** The UHD 770 handles H.265 decode, but 16 simultaneous 4K tiles were not
   tested. The GPU estimate is not a quote.
10. **Detection throughput, thermals, and noise.** The 80 inferences/s and 26 TOPS figures are
    arithmetic, not measurements. I did not run Frigate, the Hailo runtime, or a 16-stream
    load. The idle and load power figures are estimates. The case airflow and noise are unknown.
11. **UPS runtime and NUT behavior.** The runtime curve is from vendor and community data, not
    a test. NUT's `usbhid-ups` driver is reported to work with this model, but the shutdown
    threshold should be set from a measured runtime, not from the LCD percentage.
12. **The N4 back M.2 access.** The board has two M.2 slots on the back. I could not confirm
    that they are reachable without removing the motherboard from the tray. Install the OS NVMe
    and Hailo-8 before mounting the board.

13. **The printed wall bracket.** The PETG bracket for the 1U 24-port switch and patch panel has
    not been designed or tested. The switch is heavier than the old 10-inch panel; use a metal
    shelf or wall bracket if the print sags.
